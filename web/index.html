The shelf

A dozen tiny utilities, folded into one panel.

Krovi gathers the tools you reach for all day behind a single hotkey, so you stop juggling scattered apps and browser tabs. Everything is keyboard-driven and glanceable, and every tool works offline.

Clipboard history

A searchable history of recent copies — text, images and links — with pin-to-top and type filters. Re-copying promotes an item instead of duplicating it.

Paste stack

Nine numbered slots you actively push and pop — a LIFO workbench for repetitive paste workflows, kept distinct from the passive history.

Timers, stopwatch & Pomodoro

Run several countdowns and a stopwatch, or switch to work/break Pomodoro cycles on the same engine. Notified the moment time's up.

File shelf

Drag files in to park them for a minute or an hour; drag them back out into any app when you need them. Name shelves for different jobs.

Media now-playing

Reads the Windows media session to show the current track with play, pause, next and previous — no audio is captured, ever.

Weather

Current conditions and a short local forecast from a keyless public API and your Windows location. No account, nothing stored in the cloud.

Calendar & reminders

A month view with lightweight local reminders that notify you on time — the appointments and nudges that don't deserve a heavyweight calendar app.

Quick notes & to-do

World clocks

An expression calculator plus offline unit conversion and currency via cached keyless rates — numbers sorted without leaving the panel.

A closer look

Built for the way you actually work.

Each tool is small on purpose — but designed, not dashed off. Here's what a few of them do, and why they earn their spot on the shelf.

Copy & paste, upgraded

Two kinds of clipboard, for two kinds of work.

Your clipboard history quietly keeps your recent copies — text, images and links — so the thing you copied five minutes ago isn't gone. Search it, filter by type, and pin the clips you reuse.

The paste stackKrovi original is a deliberate, numbered workbench: push snippets into slots 1–9 and pop them in order. Perfect for filling a form or templating the same handful of values again and again.

  • Configurable cap; re-copying promotes instead of duplicating
  • Pinned clips float to the top and are never evicted
  • Images stored as local files, referenced from the database
Focus & time

Countdowns, a stopwatch, and a Pomodoro loop.

Set as many timers as you like — tea, a meeting hard-stop, laundry — and a stopwatch with laps alongside them. Flip the same engine into Pomodoro mode and it runs work/break cycles and counts your sessions.

Under the hood the timer logic takes an injected clock, so it's deterministic and fully unit-tested — the Pomodoro state machine alone has seven tests behind it.

  • Multiple concurrent countdowns + a lap stopwatch
  • Work/break cycles with a session counter
  • A clear notification the instant a timer finishes
Hold this for a sec

A shelf for files, and the controls for your music.

The file shelf is a holding area for drag-and-drop: pull a file in from Explorer to park it, then drag it out into an email, a chat or another folder later. Give each shelf a name for a different task.

Media now-playing reads the Windows media session to show whatever's playing — track, artist, and play / pause / next / previous — without ever touching the audio itself.

  • Drag files in and out via native OS drag-and-drop
  • Parked files keep their name, size and added-at
  • Transport controls only — no audio is captured
The glanceable stuff

Weather you can trust, a calendar that nudges you.

Weather shows current conditions and a short local forecast using a keyless public API and your Windows location — just a coarse latitude/longitude, no account, nothing kept in the cloud. It's one of only two things Krovi ever fetches.

The calendar & reminders give you a simple month view and local reminders that fire a notification on time — stored on your machine like everything else.

  • Keyless weather; coarse location only; opt-out any time
  • Local reminders with on-time notifications
  • Everything works offline except the live forecast
Think & act fast

Notes, tasks, and a command palette to run it all.

Quick notes autosave as you type, and the to-do list keeps checkable tasks beside them — no projects, no boards, just somewhere to put a thought or a task in two seconds.

The command paletteKrovi original is the keyboard spine of the shelf: one search box to launch an installed app, jump to a tool, or run a Krovi action. It's how power users will live in Krovi without touching the mouse.

  • Autosaving notes; checkable, reorderable to-dos
  • Fuzzy search across apps and Krovi actions
  • Everything reachable from the keyboard
Local by design

See where your day went — only you can.

Screen-time tracking samples which app is in the foreground and rolls it up per app, per day, so you get an honest picture of your attention. It records process names and durations only — never window titles, never URLs.

And crucially: it is reported to nobody. The aggregates live in your local database and are yours to read, export or clear. This is the privacy difference in miniature — a tracker that genuinely can't track you anywhere else.

  • Per-app, per-day totals computed on-device
  • No titles, no URLs, no cloud — ever
  • Export or clear your history whenever you like
Where your data lives

Local-first, and provably so.

Krovi is built so the privacy claim is checkable, not just stated. Here's exactly where your data sits — and every outbound call the app can make, drawn to scale.

Your data stays on your device

Clipboard, notes, to-dos, reminders, shelf files and screen-time all live in one folder under your Windows profile. Nothing syncs; there is no cloud copy.

About

An original “everything shelf”.

Krovi is an original, clean-room take on the quick-access dropdown utility — in the spirit of menu-bar and notch panels, but with its own name, design language, left tool rail, palette and icons. It is not a fork, re-skin or reverse-engineering of any existing product; the design, copy and all code are original.

It's built Windows-first on Tauri 2 with a small, testable Rust core and a Svelte 5 front end, deliberately keeping a tiny dependency surface so the privacy posture stays easy to audit. Krovi is early in development and built in the open — the source, roadmap and progress are all public. Want the detail? Head to the documentation.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

A few quick ones here; the docs FAQ goes deeper.

Is Krovi really free?

Yes — free and open source under the MIT licence, with no key, trial, paywall or “pro” tier. Every feature is available to everyone.

What are the keyboard shortcuts?

Summon or dismiss the panel with a global hotkey (default Ctrl + Alt + Space), which you can rebind in settings. You can also open it by clicking a configurable screen hot-corner, and dismiss with Esc, a click away, or the hotkey again.

The full shortcut table lives in the documentation.

Does Krovi send my data anywhere?

No. Your clipboard, notes, shelf files and screen-time stay in a local folder on your machine. The only outbound calls are optional weather and currency fetches, plus a manual update check against GitHub — all documented in the privacy section and the docs.

Which platforms are supported?

Windows only for now. The Rust core is kept portable so other platforms are possible later, but there's no macOS, Linux or mobile build today.

How does it compare to paid alternatives like Vayli?

Krovi's differentiators are being free, open source and verifiably private. For an honest, clean-room feature-by-feature breakdown — including where more mature paid apps may still be ahead — see the comparison page.

Can I contribute?

Yes. Krovi is built in the open on GitHub. Issues, ideas and pull requests are welcome as the core stabilises.
